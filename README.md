## Derivatives are where the money is, so venue choice matters more than it used to

In the first quarter of 2026, spot trading across the market ran to roughly $1.94 trillion while derivatives did about $18.63 trillion — a ratio of about 9.6 to 1. Perpetuals and futures aren't a side product anymore; they are the main event.

That scale explains the crowding at the top. Binance alone did about $4.90 trillion in derivatives volume in Q1 2026, roughly 34.9% of the top-ten total, and about 2.2 times OKX in second place. Liquidity at the very top is concentrated in a way that matters if you're trading size on BTC. It matters much less if your edge lives in a $300 million market-cap token, because that's a completely different question.

## The four numbers that decide the answer

Most "best exchange" comparisons rank platforms on a single headline fee. That number is a starting point, not a verdict. Four things do the real work:

**What you pay at your volume.** Entry-tier rates are nearly identical across the big centralized venues. The ladder you'll actually climb is what differentiates them.

**Which contracts you need.** Binance offers the widest combination of perpetuals and dated futures. Bybit carries the broadest combined contract selection among CEXs. Gate carries a perpetual list that goes deeper into mid- and small-cap tokens than most rivals.

**Depth where you trade.** CoinMarketCap's Q1 2026 figures showed Binance holding $284 million of average two-sided depth within ±1% on BTC futures, against $160 million at OKX and $76.55 million at Bybit — a gap that shows up as slippage on large orders.

**What you can do with margin.** Cross versus isolated, portfolio margin, multi-asset collateral, tokenized-equity contracts. This is where venues diverge most and where most comparison tables go quiet.

**The cost nobody puts in the comparison table**

Whatever you pay in commission, funding is usually the bigger number if you hold. Gate perpetuals settle funding every 8 hours on BTC, though individual contracts can run 1-hour, 4-hour or 8-hour cycles. Funding isn't revenue for Gate — it's a payment between longs and shorts, and it's calculated on position value, not on your margin. A position held for three days crosses nine settlements.

Two mechanics worth internalising: trading fees apply when you open, reduce or close, and never on cancelled or unfilled orders. And because fees scale with notional, leverage doesn't change the fee on a given position size — but it does make that fee larger relative to the margin you posted, which is why a 0.05% taker rate feels very different at 5x and at 50x.
